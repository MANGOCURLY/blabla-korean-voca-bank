In short

9. Minors

The service is for people learning Korean, including students of a Blabla Korean class. It is not a social network and users cannot contact each other.

If the student is under 15, the account (Google sign-in) must be opened with the holder of parental authority. The teacher may prepare the invitation as part of the class, with that holder’s agreement.

What French law actually says (keep this whichever option you retain)

  • Article 45 of the French Data Protection Act sets 15 as the age at which a minor may consent alone to processing, for an information-society service offered directly to a child, where processing is based on consent.
  • Under 15, that consent must be joint: the child and the holder(s) of parental authority. Express agreement from one holder is enough; the other is presumed to agree, but must be able to object (CNIL, recommendation 4).
  • The processing in this policy is based mainly on article 6.1.b (providing the requested service), not on consent. The article 45 age of 15 therefore does not apply in the same way.
  • Separately, a child under 15 does not, as a rule, have civil capacity to enter into the terms of use alone. That is why a holder of parental authority must be involved if option B is used.

We do not verify age automatically.
